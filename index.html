The input

It begins with heat they barely added

Every nation in this story contributes a negligible share of global greenhouse gas emissions. Yet the emissions don't stay where they're made. Atmospheric heat is not a local phenomenon.

Note the internal range: Palau and New Caledonia are outliers with large industrial or tourism-driven footprints. Most island nations emit under 4 tCO₂e per person — less than a transatlantic flight for a European tourist.

The heat store

The ocean took the heat — 91% of it

When carbon dioxide traps solar radiation, the energy has to go somewhere. Scientists have measured where: 91% enters the ocean. The rest goes into the land surface, ice, and atmosphere.

The chart shows both signals — land surface temperature anomaly and sea surface temperature anomaly — for a single country. Both are rising from the same baseline, but the ocean accumulates heat more slowly and holds it longer.

Two consequences, one cause

Warm water is bigger water

Heat doesn't only melt ice. When water warms, its molecules move faster and spread further — the same volume of water takes up more space. This thermal expansion accounts for roughly half of last century's sea-level rise.

The Pacific has experienced a clear upward trend. The numbers are small in absolute metres — but for an island nation whose highest point is four metres above sea level, every centimetre is a percentage of its future.

The seawall that lived

The same heat empties the reef

Coral is not a rock. It is an animal — a colony of tiny polyps living in a symbiotic relationship with photosynthetic algae that live inside them. When water temperature rises even 1–2°C above a seasonal limit, the coral expels its algae. It turns white. Without its food source, it starves.

The Pacific has lost an estimated 50% of living coral cover since the 1980s. The GCRMN Pacific Reports document recurrent mass bleaching events aligned with the same SST anomaly peaks visible in the temperature chart.

The shaded band marks the approximate SST anomaly threshold beyond which bleaching stress becomes likely. This is a mechanism, not a model.

Not just temperature

Rain that once knew its season

Rainfall, too, is shifting — but not in one direction. Some islands are drying; some are receiving more rain. Neither outcome is safe when agricultural systems, water catchment, and infrastructure were built around a pattern that no longer holds.

Nauru's rainfall anomaly shows the most extreme drying in the region. Tonga tends wetter. Fiji and others vary year to year as cyclone tracks shift.

The mechanism

So the waves arrive unbroken

Water has risen. The reef is dying or dead. These are two separate consequences of the same heat — but they combine into something worse than either alone.

A healthy coral reef dissipates 97% of a wave's energy before it reaches the shore. The complex structures — branches, plates, ridges — create turbulence that extracts kinetic energy from breaking waves.

When reef complexity drops by 50%, the mathematical relationship is not linear. Extreme-wave events become more than 50 times more frequent at the shore.

That is the causal hinge: warm water + dead reef = unbroken waves + higher water. The coast is now open to forces it was never meant to absorb alone.

Carlot et al. 2023, Scientific Reports (Mo'orea) · Ferrario et al. 2014 (97% energy dissipation)

The physics, finally, is a person

The line is where a home was

Cyclone Winston made landfall in Fiji on 20 February 2016 — the most powerful tropical cyclone ever recorded in the Southern Hemisphere at the time of landfall.

633,584 people were directly affected.

Every mechanism described above — warmer seas feeding cyclone intensity, dead reefs offering no wave brake, raised water extending inland storm surge — converged in that one event.

Source: SPC Pacific Data Hub · VC_DSR_AFFCT indicator · verified single-event record

Data & methodology

Climate indicators

SPC Pacific Data Hub · DF_CLIMATE_CHANGE dataset (ST_ANOM, SST_ANOM, SEA_LVL, RAIN_ANOM, GHG_EMI_CAPITA). Raw CSVs parsed in-browser; no pre-baked JSON.

Disaster data

SPC · VC_DSR_AFFCT indicator only. Other affected-persons indicators in the file mix units (persons with economic loss in USD) and are not used here.

Coastline

Digital Earth Pacific · Landsat annual shorelines 1999–2023 + rates_of_change layer (10.4 M transects). Served via digitalearthpacific.org.

Coral

GCRMN Status and Trends of Coral Reefs of the Pacific, 1980–2023 (DOI 10.59387/WIUJ2936). Bleaching mechanism: Carlot et al. 2023 (Sci. Reports), Ferrario et al. 2014. USGS Mo'orea coral-cover series.

Sea-level values are reported at 0.1 m resolution and shown as a regional trend only. The coral beat references a site-level dataset (Mo'orea) + Pacific-wide GCRMN trend — not per-country coral data. All interpolations are flagged in context.
